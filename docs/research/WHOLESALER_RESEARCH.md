## Who the Wholesaler's Customers Actually Are

- **Small retailers / kiranas are the core.** Rural and small-town retailers visit wholesale markets in nearby towns themselves; distributors' direct channels reach only ~20–30% of kirana stores, leaving the rest to wholesalers. *(Springer, "Online Retailing Paired with Kirana", doi.org/10.1007/s40547-015-0057-9)*
- **The mix is wider than kiranas:** wholesale grocery sellers also serve hotels, restaurants, caterers, and institutions — canteens, hostels, offices — as distinct buyer types. *(siridhaniya.com, wholesale FMCG seller, fetched Nov 27)*
- **The trips are real costs:** kiranas coordinate across 5–10 distributors and make 3–5 hour market trips, which eB2B platforms are only beginning to reduce. *(Redseer, "The AOV Trap", Jan 2026)* A Maharashtra kirana owner describes shutting her shop 3–4 days a month to travel 60 km for stock before switching to an eB2B agent. *(The CapTable on ElasticRun, Oct 2023)*

## How the Relationship Runs Today

| Dimension | Reality |
|-----------|---------|
| **Informal and personal** | Arrangements between wholesalers and kiranas are mostly informal; cash payment common; established brands on advance/cash, rest on credit or consignment with 1-week to 2-month credit periods. *(Springer)* |
| **Trust and credit are the glue** | Kiranas win households through "personalised service and trusted credit" *(Redseer AOV Trap)* — same credit logic runs between wholesaler and retailer. |
| **Phone is already the channel** | When a delivery is delayed, owners "just call a nearby wholesaler or another kirana we know." *(kirana owner quote, Redseer AOV Trap)* The call is the existing workflow — our product's foundation, not our invention. |
| **Thin margins** | FMCG distribution runs at ~4–6% at the distributor level *(The CapTable)*. Growth must come from keeping and serving more buyers, not from raising prices. |

## The "One-Stop" Positioning

Wholesale sellers position exactly this way: everything a business buyer needs in one order, one place, with flexible payment *(siridhaniya.com)*. The voice desk strengthens it: a retailer who can ask "do you have X?" by voice and get an instant, correct answer — even for rarely-asked items — experiences the shop as one-stop **without the owner hiring more counter staff**.

## Where a Voice Order Desk Grows the Business

| # | Opportunity | Mechanism |
|---|-------------|-----------|
| **1** | **Retention at the failure moment** | Today, when stock fails, the retailer tries the neighbouring wholesaler on the same trip *(the Redseer quote shows this is normal)*. Settling the shortage on the call — or telling him before he travels — keeps the sale and the relationship. |
| **2** | **More counter capacity at peak** | Orders phoned ahead and pre-packed mean the morning rush serves more retailers per counter hour; the packer list is the mechanism (already in the build). |
| **3** | **Demand signals for stocking** | "Asked but never carried" requests tell the owner what to start stocking — revenue the shop currently never sees (see [VOICE_DATA_INSIGHTS.md](../voice-data-insights/VOICE_DATA_INSIGHTS.md)). |
| **4** | **Institutional buyers fit voice best** | Hostels, caterers and canteens place big, repeating lists on a schedule — exactly the "repeat last list with edits" flow, and exactly the buyers where a missed item costs the most. |
| **5** | **Credit handled on the call** *(backlog)* | Outstanding-balance reminders and credit-limit checks at order time — the same workflow Udyant and VaaniAI sell separately — fold naturally into the order call later. |
| **6** | **A free upsell channel** *(backlog)* | New arrivals and scheme announcements when regulars call in — the wholesaler's equivalent of VaaniAI's scheme broadcasts, without outbound campaigns. |
| **7** | **Back-in-stock pull** | Notifying the retailers who asked for an unavailable item when it returns (already in the spec) converts a failed call into the next visit. |
